# Review the Actual Visual Output

Use for visual creation or substantial redesign. For a small revision, inspect the affected passes and preserve the approved direction. This is a judgment guide, not a numerical claim of quality or an additional approval gate.

## Inspect in Separate Passes

1. **Orientation and attention.** At intended viewing size, what attracts attention first? Can the audience identify the subject and intended point or action? In a deck, review each slide and the sequence; in a video, review the opening and key beats. Do not require every information-dense tool to have one dramatic focal point.
2. **Composition and hierarchy.** Check repeated alignment axes, deliberate size differences, proximity, margins, reading order, and safe areas. Distinguish useful whitespace from accidental holes. Do not replace useful density with emptiness to make an output appear minimal.
3. **Content and evidence.** Compare against the source: complete text, exact quotations and numbers, units, dates, meaningful images, honest charts, and working controls. A polished screenshot cannot compensate for invented evidence or missing required content.
4. **Subject and reference fit.** For an original concept, test whether the design could belong unchanged to any competitor. For reference-led work, compare the agreed structural invariants, crop, relative scale, layering, and material language. Judge against the requested degree of similarity, not novelty for its own sake.
5. **Typography and imagery.** Inspect real glyph rendering, hierarchy, wraps, line lengths, contrast, image crop, resolution, aspect ratio, and consistent visual treatment. Verify important Chinese text directly. Use images because they explain the subject, not to meet a quota. Check asset provenance and usage conditions.
6. **Continuity and variation.** Does the idea and visual system survive beyond the opening? Vary structure only when content or pacing benefits; repeated rows, slides, or panels are appropriate when they improve comparison. Avoid both repetitive decoration and arbitrary novelty.
7. **Real use.** Check the relevant states and viewing conditions below. Inspect for occlusion, clipping, accidental overlap, and important information hidden by transitions or interaction.

## Choose the Medium

| Medium | Evidence and conditions to inspect |
| --- | --- |
| Website or app | Actual rendered desktop and mobile views; intermediate width when layout changes; real tasks and navigation; relevant loading, empty, error, focus, and selected states. A mobile composition needs usable priorities and controls, not just smaller desktop elements. |
| PPT or document | Real output rendered by the relevant engine; source-content comparison; intended viewing distance, page density, line breaks, and font substitution. For editable or animated decks, keep object/editability, target-editor save/reopen, and slideshow checks distinct. |
| Image or poster | Actual output at full size and intended display size; exact requested text, subject and reference fidelity, dimensions, crop, safe areas, and alpha when transparency is required. |
| Video or motion | Actual playback plus critical frames; timing, continuity, intended motion, readability duration, transitions, audio synchronization when applicable, and loop boundary when requested. A contact sheet cannot prove playback behavior. |

## Review and Revise

- Report observations with location and impact, such as "slide 4's source line clips at the bottom," rather than "needs more polish."
- Fix required-content, fidelity, functionality, and legibility failures before aesthetic details. Identify the three most consequential remaining issues per round at most; re-render or replay after changes.
- Recheck invariants after each change: a stronger title must not cover content, a mobile fix must not break desktop, and a new animation must not hide the initial state.
- Stop when the acceptance criteria are met. If a tool or renderer is unavailable, state precisely which verification remains incomplete; do not call a simulated preview an actual rendering.
- Do not import universal quotas for photos, motion, font ratios, layout variety, or decoration. Suitability depends on audience, content, medium, reference contract, and real task.
